Flood level sources: what the NWS and NOAA tags mean
NWS NWS forecast office flood levels
Set locally by the National Weather Service office for that stretch of coast. Each gauge names its office. These are the levels the office uses when it issues Coastal Flood Advisories and Warnings, and the flood stages on NWS river hydrographs. Rivers have NWS levels only.
    NOAA NOAA derived flood levels
    Calculated by NOAA's National Ocean Service with one nationwide formula from each tide gauge's tide range (NOAA Technical Report NOS CO-OPS 086, 2018). Used in NOAA's high-tide flooding reports and outlooks. No forecast office sets them. Where they are lower than the NWS levels (as on most of the Oregon coast), they turn orange sooner.
    sources differ
    The two sources put the same Reading at different levels. The color shown is the higher of the two, so neither agency's level is hidden, and the chip names the source it came from. Before acting, confirm with the forecast office named on that gauge which level it is working from.
    "none published for this gauge"
    NOAA's station records hold no levels from that source. For NWS, ask the named office whether it uses a local level.
    Readings from NOAA CO-OPS, NOAA NDBC, Scripps CDIP, USGS (via NWS NWPS). Thresholds are the operators' published levels. Tide water levels are feet above MLLW; river levels are gage stage in feet. Times are local to the area, with the zone shown. Not an official warning product.
